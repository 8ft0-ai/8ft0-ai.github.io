01Read properlyq-fin.TR

Calibration of Passive Market Impact with a Queue-Sensitive Propagator Model

Unlike a model that only prices liquidity-taking market orders, the proposed propagator adds state-dependent queue-weighted order-flow imbalance to estimate the price response to limit-order provision. Its observable signals create a precise reading question for execution simulators: which queue states change passive fills, impact estimates and subsequent mark-out?

02Read properlycs.MA

Test-Time Communication in Trading Agent Swarms

The authors compare isolation and communication variants for 200 agents from four model families over seven US trading days and report convergence of positions and wording in some groups. The decisive point is their explicit no-peer baseline: correlated inputs can produce herding without message passing. Read for a testable diversification-risk protocol, not a trading-return advantage.

arXiv:2610.37448Evidence: Abstract + metadataExplore paper ↗
03Skim with interestq-fin.TR

Do Round Numbers Hold Prices? Occupation Densities and Depth-Conditioned Hazards in the Limit Order Book

The abstract distinguishes mechanical queue-depth concentration from a behavioural price barrier, finding no longer residence at round levels after conditioning on depth in five Nasdaq stocks. Skim the conditioning strategy and think about false support/resistance signals that are really liquidity-state effects.

arXiv:2610.34787Evidence: Abstract + metadataExplore paper ↗
04Save for laterq-fin.TR

Optimal Order Placement under a State-Dependent Hawkes Process Limit Order Book model

A state-dependent Hawkes order-flow model with inventory penalties offers a useful possible simulator control for limit-quote selection. The abstract's comparison is deliberately nuanced: its proposed placement beats one- and three-tick fixed-spread wealth baselines but not two ticks. Save for a later market-making research design with matched inventory objectives.

02 / Editorial themes

Signals in the small sample

Qualitative themes from the weekly report, not measured trends across all arXiv papers.

01

Queue-aware execution

passive orders create state-dependent price responses; fixed-spread and fixed-impact assumptions may conceal exposure in realistic market-making evaluation.

02

Independent agents can become correlated

agent messages, shared market feeds and aligned prompts can each cause position convergence; causal claims need an isolated no-peer baseline.

03

Explain price barriers

round-number depth concentration need not imply an independent price barrier once available liquidity is conditioned upon.

04

Portfolio risk versus alpha

lower volatility from sparse representations does not automatically beat a simple risk-adjusted contrarian baseline.
