Overview

Unlike a model that only prices liquidity-taking market orders, the proposed propagator adds state-dependent queue-weighted order-flow imbalance to estimate the price response to limit-order provision. Its observable signals create a precise reading question for execution simulators: which queue states change passive fills, impact estimates and subsequent mark-out?

What the researchers found

Author-reported results

  • Author-reported Table 4: for E-mini S&P 500 futures in 2025, mean daily out-of-sample R² rises from 0.458 for the propagator baseline to 0.603 for the full queue-state dictionary, a gain of 0.145 R² units—not a 14.5% trading return.
  • Across the 12 equity-index instrument-year comparisons in Table 4, the reported full-dictionary lift ranges from approximately 0.06 to 0.19 in mean daily out-of-sample R². Those gains belong to a richer state dictionary, not solely to a single passive-order coefficient.

Source: original manuscript v1, Section 4.4, Table 4 (mean daily out-of-sample R²).

Why it matters — Quant Radar interpretation

A model of signed trades alone can miss information about the liquidity a trade encounters. In research, the defensible implication is to compare queue-sensitive explanations with a simpler flow baseline on held-out periods—not to assume that a better price fit translates into better fills or execution profits.

Method and research question

The authors extend an aggressive-order-flow propagator by adding a queue-sensitive price-response component. They estimate Hawkes-driven market-order flow and queue-reactive order placement and cancellations, then regress price changes on the propagator and queue-weighted imbalance signals. The empirical comparison uses order-book messages from equity-index futures over 2023–2025 and Treasury futures in 2025, with time-ordered rolling out-of-sample tests against a classical propagator baseline.

Source: manuscript v1, Sections 2–4 (price decomposition, queue calibration and out-of-sample regressions).

Material limitations

The calibration uses specific futures, order-book reconstruction, price proxies and filtered regular sessions; the authors explicitly exclude some irregular and stressed periods. The full dictionary also adds flexibility and selected time scales. Its out-of-sample R² improvement does not demonstrate a deployable passive-order execution advantage, robust realised fills or net P&L.

How to test the claim

Can a pre-registered, time-split comparison of a classical propagator and a queue-weighted variant retain its out-of-sample price-change improvement during excluded or stressed sessions, after controlling for added model complexity? Propose this as an offline falsification test only.
