Overview

The abstract distinguishes mechanical queue-depth concentration from a behavioural price barrier, finding no longer residence at round levels after conditioning on depth in five Nasdaq stocks. Skim the conditioning strategy and think about false support/resistance signals that are really liquidity-state effects.

What the researchers found

Author-reported results

  • Author-reported Section 6.2, Table 3: Intel round-dollar mean-duration indices after depth adjustment are 0.94 [0.90, 0.98] at the best bid and 0.93 [0.89, 0.97] at the best ask; SoFi's bid and ask indices are both 0.93 [0.90, 0.96]. These comparisons indicate approximately 6–7% shorter, not longer, durations than matched control levels in those stocks.
  • Section 6.3 reports more displayed depth at round-dollar quotes in all five stocks: Intel has bid and ask depth ratios of 1.47, and SoFi ratios of 1.50 and 1.46. Section 6.2 separately reports directional asymmetry for Intel: 54% of single-tick departures are upward when the round dollar is the best bid versus 46% when it is the best ask. Depth concentration, residence time and departure direction are different empirical quantities.

Source: original manuscript v1, Sections 6.2–6.4, Tables 3–4 (depth-adjusted durations, directions and liquidity concentration).

Why it matters — Quant Radar interpretation

A round-number liquidity concentration is not evidence of a price level holding longer once entry-queue depth is matched. The authors' Intel/SoFi findings instead distinguish shorter residence from a directional tendency to depart away from the round number. A support/resistance feature or execution simulator should separately test depth, duration and directional hazard rather than treating a round-dollar flag as a durable barrier or a trading signal.

Method and research question

The authors analyse 250 trading days in 2025 for Apple, Cisco, Intel, SoFi and American Airlines using Nasdaq TotalView-ITCH best-quote/depth data. They define a price-level visit using one-tick-spread quote states, mark censored exits, and separately fit depth-adjusted departure-duration hazards and upward/downward departure probabilities. Comparators include other price levels, distance-to-round-number controls and placebo levels three cents above a round dollar; models control for entry depth, stock/day/time of day and other observed quote-state covariates.

Source: manuscript v1, Sections 2.1–2.3 and 4.3 (one-tick quote visits, depth-conditioned duration and direction models).

Material limitations

This is a 2025, five-stock best-quote study rather than a universal market mechanism. One-tick-spread observation rules censor or exclude some periods; Apple has a materially smaller one-tick-spread share and its duration estimate is filter-sensitive. Contemporaneous queue depth is endogenous to departures, so the primary model uses entry depth and separate diagnostics. Hidden liquidity, alternate tick sizes and a causal or profitable trading response are not established by the reported indices.

How to test the claim

In an offline, time-split ITCH fixture for one of the five named stocks, pre-register the hypothesis that round-dollar visits have longer residence after matching entry bid/ask depth and time-of-day. Compare the duration index and its day-clustered interval against ordinary levels and a +$0.03 placebo, with a separate upward-departure log-odds contrast. The barrier hypothesis fails if the held-out duration index is not credibly above 1 after the controls, even if depth is higher; report directional effects separately. No experiment is claimed to have been run.
