Overview

Practical root-cause audit of six live-versus-replay mismatches, not a claim of profitable trading.

What the researchers found

Author-reported results

  • For one reported 09:30 ET stock-minute, the cached feed showed roughly 7,900 shares against about 3.4 million on the consolidated feed—approximately 0.2% of reference volume. This is a single data-quality example, not an estimated portfolio return.
  • The authors found 20 of 61 simulated fills sized beyond a limit the live account enforced; they also report one adjusted-price mismatch affecting 37 of 98 symbols and an audit of 53 production-read configuration keys absent from the declared config.

Source: original manuscript v1, Section 2 (data, code and configuration defects).

Why it matters — Quant Radar interpretation

The common failure is that data, clocks, risk limits and settings follow different paths in research and execution. Agreement in aggregate backtest metrics cannot replace a decision-by-decision parity check over the exact production information and order path.

Method and research question

The authors compare real intraday US-equity system behaviour with a backtest replay of the same days, tracing six observed defects to three mismatches in data, two in code and one in configuration. They compare feed completeness, adjusted versus live price basis, required inputs, position sizing, exchange-time interpretation and whether enabled settings actually reach the order path.

Source: manuscript v1, Sections 1–3 (live-versus-replay defect cases and controls).

Material limitations

This is a case study of one system, vendor and account, not a defect-rate estimate for quant systems generally. The authors note that a missing-input defect affected much of the reported parity harness history; its effect is not fully measured. The study makes no profitability claim, and no independent access to operational logs was established here.

How to test the claim

Can a deterministic, no-trading parity fixture replay a single stock-day with identical feed identifier, raw/adjusted price basis, exchange-time conversion, all required inputs and post-scaling risk caps, then fail on the first divergence? Treat the checklist as a proposed verifier, not as evidence of live safety.
