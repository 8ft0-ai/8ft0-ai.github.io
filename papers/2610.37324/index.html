Overview

A state-dependent Hawkes order-flow model with inventory penalties offers a useful possible simulator control for limit-quote selection. The abstract's comparison is deliberately nuanced: its proposed placement beats one- and three-tick fixed-spread wealth baselines but not two ticks. Save for a later market-making research design with matched inventory objectives.

What the researchers found

Author-reported results

  • Author-reported Section 5.5.4, Table 12: after daily inventory liquidation at the closing mid-price, adaptive DPP aggregate terminal wealth is US$3,165,081; fixed two-tick quotes yield US$3,443,368, or 8.8% more relative to DPP. The one-tick and three-tick fixed benchmarks yield US$2,549,852 and US$2,695,842 respectively. These are simulated wealth figures, not live returns.
  • Sections 5.5.1 and 6 report that the adaptive strategy posted at two ticks in 71.52% of ask intervals and 77.33% of bid intervals. Absolute inventory remained within the 5,000-share soft threshold during 47.09% of sampled intervals and peaked at 6,311.2 shares. The soft rule does not enforce a hard inventory limit.

Source: original manuscript v1, Sections 5.5.3–5.5.4, Tables 11–12 (five-day wealth and fixed-spread benchmark comparison).

Why it matters — Quant Radar interpretation

The intuitive claim that adaptive quotes beat a simple fixed-spread control is not supported by the paper's reported unpenalised wealth ranking: fixed two-tick placement wins on that measure. The economically relevant unresolved comparison is whether inventory-aware quoting improves the authors' inventory-penalised objective or reduces adverse inventory exposure. The displayed wealth totals cannot answer that without pre-liquidation inventory for every comparator.

Method and research question

The authors use five regular-session MSFT trading days, 3–7 November 2014, comprising 117,000 one-second intervals of LOBSTER event data. A state-dependent Hawkes arrival model supplies order-count probabilities; block-specific linear demand estimates and inventory-penalised dynamic programming determine inventory-sensitive quote offsets. The forward simulation rounds and clips quotes to one through five ticks and compares against fixed one-, two- and three-tick quotes using the same observed order-arrival sequence and demand coefficients.

Source: manuscript v1, Sections 2–3 and 5.1, 5.4 (state-dependent Hawkes counts, demand calibration and dynamic programming).

Material limitations

The sample is one stock for five trading days in 2014, with executions produced by calibrated linear demand rather than verified exchange fills. Section 5.4.3 adds soft inventory feedback, rounding and clipping after the theoretical control derivation, so its optimality proof does not establish optimality of the implemented rule. Table 12 omits the quadratic terminal inventory penalty, and cross-side demand moments use a product independence approximation; neither the risk-adjusted ranking nor out-of-sample net execution advantage is established.

How to test the claim

For a frozen, no-trading replay of the same 117,000 one-second MSFT intervals, apply adaptive and fixed two-tick quotes to identical arrival inputs and demand coefficients. Record each strategy's pre-liquidation terminal inventory, closing-mid wealth, the same penalty lambda times squared pre-liquidation inventory and intraday maximum absolute exposure. Compare day-level penalised payoff and inventory tails with a common lambda and sensitivity checks; a claimed risk-adjusted advantage fails if adaptive quoting cannot improve the penalised payoff without worse inventory excursions under matched assumptions. This is a proposed falsification design only.
