Overview

Trading code that executes successfully can still violate the requested stops, cooldowns or position state.

Methods & research question

MintEval generates reference trading programs from composable signal, filter, sizing and risk blocks, translates them into colloquial desk instructions and asks models to implement the same behaviour. Reference and generated programs are run bar by bar on identical BTCUSDT 15-minute data and frictions. Its primary ActionMatch measures agreement on active bars; exact task equivalence, trade matching and silent failures are assessed separately.

Original manuscript v1, Sections 2–4 (paired reference programs, action metrics and task generation). This explanatory reading does not establish independent validation.

Evidence and judgement

Canonical source-report assessment: Abstract + metadata

Reader V1.2 separately inspected selected sections of the original arXiv manuscript HTML (v1) to prepare an explanatory note. The source report's earlier evidence classification remains unchanged; original author results are not independently replicated, and code, data, trading edge and execution claims remain unverified.

Findings reported by the authors

  • The authors report 800 tasks overall. On a stratified 200-task subset, their frontier-model evaluation reached mean ActionMatch 0.889 and exact reproduction 57.5%, yet 27.5% of tasks were 'silent failures': runnable code with ActionMatch below the paper's 0.9 convention.
  • In the closed diagnostic setting, 79.2% of implementations whose specifications were correctly interpreted still disagreed with their references on more than 10% of active bars. The benchmark's tested LLM judge accepted the silent failures, according to the authors.

Source: original manuscript v1, Section 5 (model comparisons and silent failures). These are author-reported observations, not Quant Radar calculations.

Research significance (editorial interpretation)

A plausible equity curve, passing compile step or successful verbal paraphrase does not establish that a generated strategy actually implements its stated risk policy. Action-level trace parity is a more direct audit target than profit or code-text similarity, especially for persistent state and stops.

Limitations & unresolved gates

The tasks are programmatically generated and benchmarked on one market/time frame and an engine-specific interface. The 0.9 silent-failure cut-off is a stated convention; the 200-task model result cannot be extrapolated to all 800 tasks or to live broker execution. No independent reproduction of the benchmark is claimed here.

Research actions

Could a frozen offline suite compare a known reference strategy and one generated implementation on identical bars, costs and state transitions, including breakeven stops and cooldown resets, and report active-bar ActionMatch plus the first mismatching decision? This is a proposed test, not an authorised run.

This is a research question, not permission to perform an experiment or change screening, publication, backlog or trading state.
