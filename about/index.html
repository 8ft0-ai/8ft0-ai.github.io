About / Research methodology

Careful reading, clear limitations.

What the research found. Why it matters. How to test the claim.

Independent interpretation of quantitative research, with explicit evidence boundaries and research questions designed to be challenged.

Quant Radar is a static editorial reading guide to quantitative-finance research, published by the GitHub account 8ft0-ai. It helps readers decide what merits further investigation. It is not a journal, an independent replication service, investment advice or a live trading system.

The featured-article editorial standard

Finding: What did the researchers test, against which comparator, on what sample, and what did they report? Numerical findings are attributed to the authors and are not described as independently verified without actual replication.

Significance: What practical consequence follows for model evaluation, portfolio research, execution or risk? An editorial inference is identified as an interpretation, not a result demonstrated by the paper.

Test: Which bounded, falsifiable research question would challenge the claim, using defined inputs, controls, metrics and a failure condition? Proposed research steps do not authorise execution.

Papers lacking enough evidence to answer these questions remain concise reading-index entries, not featured technical analyses. Article length is not a proxy for technical quality.

Selection and editorial judgement

The current reader presents two distinct report-derived selections: five individually assessed papers from the qualified partial 2026 Week 44 reading brief, and 14 individually discussed papers from the qualified, incomplete October 2026 digest. Seventeen unique base arXiv IDs appear across the two selections; cross-report editorial dispositions remain separate.

The weekly reading actions are Read properly, Skim with interest, Save for later and Ignore unless this becomes relevant. The monthly editorial groupings are Priority Read, Other Notable, Watchlist and Skipped / Low-Priority. Neither selection represents comprehensive arXiv screening or a measured publication trend.

What the evidence establishes

The canonical weekly and monthly editorial judgements remain at Abstract + metadata assessment depth. Reader V1.2 additionally consulted selected original manuscript sections for five existing priority papers, with version-pinned author-reported results and separate editorial interpretation. This is a supplementary manuscript reading, not a systematic full-paper critical audit, an independent replication or an upgrade to the original report's assessment depth. No code/data reproduction, independent backtest, verified transaction costs, leakage control or profitable performance is claimed. Missing information remains unresolved / not established, not an automatic pass or confirmed failure.

A recommendation to read signals editorial interest in a question or technique. It does not establish scientific validity or authorise a trading experiment.

Public notes and private canonical records

The published Week 44 source notes and October source notes summarise the selected reading decisions and identify the original report paths and source snapshot. They are not full copies of the underlying canonical Markdown reports, which remain in a private research repository. Consequently, public readers cannot independently reconstruct the complete internal screening and provenance process from this site.

Each paper page links to its original public arXiv abstract and PDF. The monthly and weekly labels remain distinct. Paper dates and titles are report-derived; check arXiv directly for current manuscript revisions.

Corrections and editorial responsibility

The public static publication is maintained under the 8ft0-ai account. To report an incorrect arXiv ID, attribution, broken link, misplaced editorial disposition or misleading evidence statement, open a public correction issue (a GitHub account is required). Identify the affected page and the original arXiv source. GitHub Issues are public: do not include private credentials, confidential data or personal information.

A correction should be checked against the original source and documented in a reviewed source-repository change before the static publication is rebuilt. Corrections do not directly alter canonical screening state or editorial history. No response-time commitment is implied.

Access and current acceptance limits

This site has no reader accounts, remote progress synchronisation or analytics. Reading progress is stored only in the browser in which it is marked. Search-engine indexing remains disabled during initial acceptance; a physical iPhone/Safari test and human-assisted screen-reader check have not yet been established.
